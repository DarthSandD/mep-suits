One size up, one size down — because that's the real decision

SizeID mmm/skPa / 100 mMy call

How I size pipe (CIBSE AM12 / ASHRAE behind it): 0.9–2.4 m/s where people can hear it — faster gets noisy and starts eating the pipe — and 100–350 Pa/m as the economic gradient; past ~400 Pa/m you're just buying pump energy forever. Closed loops owe nothing for height, only open systems (tanks, towers) pay for lift. And don't forget control-valve authority — add ≥ 30–50 % of the branch drop on top of index-run friction, or your valves will hunt and you'll be back on site.

Low-ΔT syndrome — I've chased this fault more than any other: measure 3–4 K across a coil designed for 6 K and your flow balloons for the same load, and pump energy climbs with the cube of that flow. Don't take my word for it — run 6 K, then run 3.5 K at the same kW above and watch the flow. That's the penalty, in numbers you can take to the client.

Fan Laws & Power

The cube law is the closest thing this trade has to free money, and this page is how I prove it to clients. Why I push VSDs on every retrofit: knock 20 % off the speed and you've halved the fan power (0.8³ = 0.51). Throttle a damper instead and you keep the speed, burn the pressure as pure loss, and save almost nothing for the same flow. One honest caveat from experience: the cube law assumes a fixed, all-friction system curve — with a big fixed static head in the system, it will flatter the savings. Check before you promise.
